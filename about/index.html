About Us

Building fairness, confidence, and better baseball—one pitch at a time.

HeyBLU exists because the strike zone has never been consistent, accessible, or easy to measure. We're a team of coaches, players, and builders who believe technology can support umpires, improve sportsmanship, and give coaches better tools to teach the game.

Our Story

HeyBLU didn't start in a lab—it started on the field. I've pitched in MSBL for years, and like a lot of players, I was vocal about bad strike zones. My daughter's last baseball at-bat at nine ended on pitches over her head, and the pattern kept repeating across youth games and adult leagues. By early 2023, when AI breakthroughs became mainstream, I found myself asking: "Why can't we solve this with technology?"

A Marin County athletic director later told me their JV teams didn't have umpires because of the shortage. That changed everything. Officials face worsening sportsmanship, low pay, and constant pressure—especially the youngest umpires. Many quit within two seasons. I realized this wasn't just about accuracy; it was about supporting the people keeping the game running.

So we built HeyBLU: an AI-powered umpire and pitch-tracking system that runs on a single iPhone. It delivers a repeatable strike zone, real-time ball–strike calls, and pitch-location data coaches can use immediately. And for umpires—especially new and youth officials—we're committed to offering HeyBLU for free to help them train, gain confidence, and face less abuse.

We're building this for coaches, but we're also building it for the blue.

JP is a 20-year global sales and staffing leader with experience at WeWork, 24 Hour Fitness, Blend, GoPro, and multiple startups, including one he founded and funded himself. He has built revenue teams, hired engineers at scale, and crafted GTM strategies for high-growth companies.

Now he helps turn that purpose into traction.

Brian Marcus

Brian and I have played on the same MSBL teams for over a decade. When I first started exploring whether a single camera could reliably call balls and strikes, I asked Brian—an IP attorney—if he thought it was even possible. At first, he wasn't convinced. Brian brings rare sports-tech IP expertise. His firm handled MLB's first strike zone patent. He personally worked on the NFL's yellow first-down marker patent. He has filed all BLU patents and trademarks, and guides our long-term defensibility.
