Games, scrimmages & who’s actually calling balls and strikes

The umpire shortage is real. Leagues lean on teen umpires (13, 14, and up) who grew up on phones and will happily use tech. They lean on parents and volunteers who said yes once and keep getting asked back. They lean on coaches umpiring someone else’s game because nobody else showed up.

HeyBLU puts plate-anchored 3D tracking on a single iPhone at the backstop — real ball flight in real time, processed on the device — so the person behind the plate has objective zone feedback while they learn the job or grind through a doubleheader.

What HeyBLU does at the plate

  • Live Strike/Ball audio when the batter takes the pitch — instant feedback for everyone on the field.
  • Pitch crossing location logged on swings too — the zone dot and session data still capture where the ball crossed the plate when the trajectory is trusted.
  • 3D geometry locked to home plate — the zone moves with the real dish, not a flat overlay on video.

Built for the umps you actually have

  • Youth umpire programs — 13- and 14-year-olds (and older) who want a confidence boost and instant feedback between innings.
  • Parent & volunteer umps — objective help on the corner without years of plate experience.
  • Coach-umpires — when the league rotates who must work the other team’s game and you still have to call a fair zone.

What the plate umpire still owns

Calling balls and strikes is only part of the job. Checked swings, catcher’s interference, balks, time, runners, equipment, and game flow stay with the human. HeyBLU goes deep on where the baseball crossed the strike zone in 3D — call the pitch, hear HeyBLU, compare, adjust.

For a 15-year-old working their fifth game of the weekend, that loop builds the zone faster than guessing alone.
