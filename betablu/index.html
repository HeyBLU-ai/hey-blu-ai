The Basics

HeyBLU is an iPhone app that uses your camera and Augmented Reality (AR) to track a pitch in 3D and call ball or strike. By measuring exactly where the ball crosses the front of the plate relative to the strike zone, it provides an audible and on-screen call for each pitch that isn't swung at. It relies on precise 3D location, not a human umpire's eye.

No. HeyBLU only evaluates pitches that are not swung at. If the batter swings, the human umpire (or coach) makes the call. The app does not attempt to decide whether a batter checked their swing or made contact.

When setting up the app, you will choose an age group (e.g., Pro, 14U, 12U, 10U) and a mound distance (e.g., 45 ft for youth leagues or 60.5 ft for regulation). These settings determine the specific width, height, and location of the strike zone. Make sure these settings match the game you are tracking!

Devices & Environment

HeyBLU requires an iPhone capable of handling 60fps ARKit and advanced computer vision models. Because of this, the minimum requirement is an iPhone 11 or later. However, for the absolute best performance and processing speed, we highly recommend using an iPhone 12 or newer.

Note: Please tell us your exact iPhone model when you apply or provide feedback so we can track device-specific performance.

To get the most accurate calls, HeyBLU requires specific conditions. This is what the app is currently built and tested for:

  • Outdoor baseball fields in daylight: Good lighting is crucial for the camera to track the ball at high speeds.
  • A stable tripod setup: Mount the phone roughly 12–30 feet behind home plate, pointing toward the field.
  • Regulation baseballs: Scuffed or dirty baseballs are perfectly fine. We are currently trained specifically on regulation-sized baseballs (support for softballs is planned and coming soon!).

As a beta tester, you are welcome to try HeyBLU in the following environments so we can gather data, but expect it to underperform or fail:

  • Indoor cages and bullpens: Artificial lighting, walls, netting, and lack of real field depth make tracking unreliable.
  • Night games or heavy shade: The cameras currently struggle with high-speed tracking in low light.
  • Hand-held use: If the phone moves during a pitch, the AR "world" drifts, resulting in highly inaccurate calls. A tripod or fixed fence mount is mandatory.
  • Non-regulation balls: Softballs, training softies, or wiffle balls may not track correctly just yet.

Setup & Installation

  1. We will send you a TestFlight link via email.
  2. Open the email on your iPhone.
  3. Download the TestFlight app from the App Store (if you don't already have it).
  4. Tap the link in our email to install "HeyBLU" (or "BLU").

(Note: You will not find the beta by searching the public App Store. If the link doesn't work, reply to our email with your iPhone model and iOS version).

Do not skip these steps! Proper setup is the most important factor in getting accurate calls:

  1. Scan the area (Most Important!): Your job here is to calibrate the field and essentially "train the umpire" to know the exact location of the zone via a scan. When the scan is done right, HeyBLU just works. Follow the on-screen instructions to scan the area around home plate with your iPhone. Take your time; this allows the app to build a precise 3D map of the environment.
  2. Place the zone: Position the virtual strike zone on or just above home plate when prompted.
  3. Mount the phone: Place the phone on a tripod or fixed mount so it does not move during the game.
  4. Tap "Play Ball": HeyBLU will only start calling pitches once you tap this button.

Troubleshooting

HeyBLU is designed to stay silent rather than guess. It only announces a call when it is highly confident in the ball's trajectory. If the ball is blocked from the camera's view (by the catcher, umpire, or a net) for too much of its flight, or if the tracking quality was poor, it may stay silent or show a warning.

The app constantly measures if the phone has moved since you locked in the strike zone. If the phone shifts, gets bumped, or vibrates in the wind, it experiences "drift." When this happens, calls become unreliable.

Fix: Ensure your mount is completely tightened and stable. Avoid bumping the tripod. If it's a windy day, try to weigh down the tripod or find a more sheltered spot.

As baseball people, we often say about human umpires: "He might be bad, but at least he's consistent." If you notice HeyBLU is making consistent misses just outside or inside, it usually means your camera isn't perfectly centered behind the plate. The good news is, unlike a human umpire, HeyBLU is easy to fix!

Just tap the Settings (gear icon) and use the "Adjust HeyBLU" (or Inside/Outside) toggle. This shifts the effective depth so the app's calls match your physical setup. Usually, bumping this adjustment a click or two in the opposite direction is all you need for the rest of the game.
