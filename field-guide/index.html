Fix a problem

No calls, or fewer calls than expected

  • Camera beyond ~15 ft? Move to 10–12 ft.
  • Check red-line framing—full pitch flight needs to be between both lines.
  • White fence or light-colored wall behind the pitcher? Move to the other side of the plate, or find a darker backdrop.
  • Batter blocking the view? A batter standing on the same side of the plate as the camera can block part of the pitch. Camera on the recommended 1st-base side means this usually shows up with left-handed batters. Shoot from the recommended ~45° angle, not head-on, to keep most of the flight visible.

Calls consistently biased one direction — inside, outside, high, or low

  • Camera angle less than ~20° from the pitch line? Reposition to the recommended ~45° offset—inside/outside accuracy suffers shooting close to head-on.
  • Try Calibrate: tap Calibrate, move the yellow dot to the correct location, and test another pitch.
  • Still off after calibrating? Check zone size—a zone too small gets more "Ball" calls, one too large gets too many "Strike" calls. Gear icon during session, or Resize Zone after session.

Calls consistently land 1–2 feet off with a batter in the box

Confirm Live At Bats was selected on the setup screen—Bullpen mode can track the batted ball instead of the pitch. Still off after switching? Calibrate on a clean pitch, or rescan.

Calls got worse partway through the session

  • Tripod bumped, or a long idle between pitches? Restart the session; rescan if anyone walked through the scene.
  • All-dirt or low-contrast field? Position tracking can drift more here—Calibrate on a clean pitch to stabilize, or rescan if it keeps happening.
  • Phone got hot in the sun? Accuracy drops when it overheats—get it into shade or use a small fan.

Zone doesn't line up with the real plate after mounting

Drag the zone back onto the plate, or tap Adjust Plate for an overhead view and use the arrows to slide it into place.

Pro iPhone: blue plate moved and shrank during the walk to the tripod

Expected behavior. Enter your exact camera-to-plate distance and lens height (tape measure) when prompted. After entering them, use Adjust Plate to slide the blue plate back onto the real one. Check it looks right — you're ready to go.

Calibrate isn't fixing it, or Apply button is grayed out

  • Only calibrate on a pitch with no contact—Apply is grayed out if there wasn't enough clean data from the last pitch.
  • One calibration on a clean pitch is usually enough. Calibrating repeatedly usually means something else is wrong.
  • Still broken? Close the app, clear the plate area, and start with a fresh scan.

Second device won't connect

Use an Android hotspot or travel router—not every Wi‑Fi works. Using someone's iPhone hotspot instead? The tracking phone needs Airplane Mode on, then Wi‑Fi back on, before it joins. Details below.

Can't add another phone

Check the antenna icon (Share Game) on the tracking phone—7/7 means you're already at the max of 7 connected devices. Disconnect one you're not using, or toggle the antenna off and back on. See Multiple second screens.

MPH is approximate (±5). Strike zone ~within one ball width when setup is good. More detail: Help & FAQ → Troubleshooting.

Setup checklist

  • Tripod mounted, 10–15 ft from the plate, offset ~45° from the pitch line (not aimed at the pitcher).
  • Camera height about mid-zone—you should see the real home plate and the highest pitch you expect in frame.
  • Ball path between the two red lines—zone on one line, mound on the other (don’t center the zone between them).
  • Virtual plate lined up on the real plate before Play Ball. If it shifts after mounting, drag it back or use Adjust Plate.
  • After Play Ball, throw a few test pitches and Calibrate as needed.

Connect two devices

Follow Game and Command Center need a network where both iPhones can find each other. Not every Wi‑Fi or hotspot works—iOS Personal Hotspot blocks device-to-device communication by default. Single-phone tracking does not need Wi‑Fi.

What works (easiest first)

Setup Result
Android phone hotspot — both iPhones join (no SIM needed) Recommended field setup
Travel router (~$20, e.g. TP-Link TL-WR802N or GL‑iNet pocket router) Best for 3+ phones—all join; no internet required
3rd-party iPhone hotspot — tracking phone: Airplane Mode → Wi‑Fi on → join first Works only with that Airplane Mode step on the tracking iPhone

What does not work

Tracking iPhone as its own hotspot Does not work
Two iPhones on same iOS hotspot without Airplane Mode + Wi‑Fi on the tracking phone Does not work

Multiple second screens (experimental)

One tracking iPhone can share live data with up to 7 other devices at once (typical: 1 Command Center + up to 6 Follow Game phones for parents or dugout coaches). Follow Game and Command Center can connect at the same time—you do not need to disconnect one for the other.

  • On the tracking phone: turn on Share Game—the antenna icon—after every device joins Wi‑Fi. The badge shows connected/7 (e.g. 2/7).
  • 1–2 second screens are field-tested. 3 or more are supported in the app but experimental—use a travel router and report issues to support@heyblu.ai.
  • Use only one Command Center per session for remote control; extra slots are for Follow Game viewers.

On each iPhone: Settings → HeyBLU → Local Network On. Full walkthrough: Help & FAQ → Connect two devices.

Before the field

Before the field
  • Volume up or Bluetooth speaker / earbuds
  • HeyBLU → Local Network On (second device)
  • Two devices? Android hotspot or travel router (recommended; travel router best for 3+ phones). iPhone hotspot: tracking phone needs Airplane Mode → Wi‑Fi on first
  • Clean camera lens
  • Tripod (inside fence); measuring tape on Pro phones
  • Charge phone; battery pack for long sessions
  • Game balls—slightly scuffed white tracks best

Limits

Works best: outdoor · 10–15 ft · daylight · ~60 MPH and under.

Expect more hassle: indoor only · much past ~15 ft · 65+ MPH untested · battery under ~30% · heavy breaking stuff still in testing.
